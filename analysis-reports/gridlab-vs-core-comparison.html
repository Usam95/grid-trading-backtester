1Executive summary

gridlab is a correctness-first rewrite of grid-backtest-core. It throws away the original's biggest liabilities — same-bar (lookahead) fills, a dual-ledger that let trades and equity disagree, a flat fee model, and close-only drawdown — and adds the things a serious grid study needs: maker/taker fees, slippage, futures (shorting, leverage, isolated-margin liquidation), walk-forward, Monte-Carlo, deflated Sharpe, an HTML report, and a clean JSON run_backtest(spec) facade that a web frontend can sit on (which is exactly what gridlab-studio does).

But the rewrite traded breadth for trustworthiness in a few places. The original's Numba JIT engine, multiprocessing + multi-timeframe parameter sweeps, order-expiry/TTL, an RSI filter, ATR-scaled stop-loss/take-profit, a results-persistence layer, and a couple of niche bootstrap/sizing behaviours were not ported. None block correct single backtests; several matter a lot once you scale to large research sweeps or want to archive runs.

One-line verdict: gridlab is the engine you should trust and build a product on. grid-backtest-core is faster at brute-force sweeps and broader in a few research conveniences — but its numbers are less defensible. The highest-value next moves are re-adding sweep parallelism/speed and a couple of cheap feature ports (RSI filter, ATR SL/TP, a results store).

3Lineage & design philosophy

grid-backtest-core (author tag Usam95) optimised for speed and feature breadth: two engines, a Numba kernel claiming ~200–400× speedups, multi-timeframe multiprocessing sweeps, and a rich dynamic-grid strategy. The cost was correctness debt and two engines that could silently disagree (the fast engine models a reduced feature set).

gridlab inverts the priorities: one engine that is provably consistent (a single Ledger is the source of truth for both trades and equity), explicit cost/risk modelling, overfitting-aware research, and a serialisable contract designed so a UI/REST layer never has to reach into engine internals. It is fewer lines across more modules — tighter, more decomposed, easier for a human or an agent to navigate.

7gridlab-only upgrades

Correctness & realism

  • Single Ledger — trades and equity provably reconcile
  • Conservative (next-bar) fill mode kills the worst lookahead bias
  • Maker/taker fees + spread/impact slippage
  • Stops fill at trigger price; gaps fill at open
  • Partial fills via volume participation
  • Intrabar drawdown + duration; corrected trade stats

Markets, research & product

  • Futures: shorting, leverage, isolated-margin liquidation
  • Walk-forward (OOS) + Monte-Carlo + deflated Sharpe
  • DCA benchmark & trailing-stop exit
  • Self-contained HTML report (no build step)
  • JSON run_backtest(spec)→dict facade → powers gridlab-studio
  • Property-based tests for cash/FIFO invariants

9Grid-trading research summary

The empirical literature and bot-vendor documentation converge on a few hard truths that directly shape the backlog above:

  • Grids harvest volatility inside a range and bleed in strong trends. Their natural habitat is sideways / mean-reverting markets; a long grid in a downtrend “buys a falling knife” and accumulates an underwater bag.
  • Costs decide marginal profitability. Hundreds of round-trips make maker/taker fees and slippage first-order — which is precisely why gridlab’s fee/slippage model matters and a flat fee misleads.
  • Robustness comes from filters + caps + adaptivity: trend/regime filters (EMA/ADX), inventory & gross-exposure caps, ATR-based spacing/exits, and walk-forward/Monte-Carlo validation. gridlab already has most of these; ATR SL/TP and the RSI filter are the cheap missing pieces.
  • Most grid backtests over-fit. A grid tuned to one window often dies out-of-sample — hence deflated Sharpe + walk-forward + Monte-Carlo are non-negotiable for a defensible result (gridlab ✓; old engine ✗).

Sources (full annotations in analysis-reports/grid-trading-research-sources.md): Investopedia — Grid Trading; Binance Academy — What Is Grid Trading; Pionex — Grid Trading Bot guide; Bailey & López de Prado — The Deflated Sharpe Ratio / The Probability of Backtest Overfitting.

10Verdict & recommended next step

Use gridlab. It is the more correct, more honest, more product-ready engine, and it already powers gridlab-studio. grid-backtest-core remains useful only as a reference for the scale features that weren’t ported (Numba speed, parallel/multi-TF sweeps) and a couple of niche knobs.

Recommended next step: ship Tier A.1 — parallelise grid_search (a 1-day win that immediately makes the Research tab usable on large grids), then the Tier B cheap ports (RSI filter, ATR SL/TP, order TTL, results store). Defer the full Numba fast engine until after a test_engine_parity.py exists, so you never re-create the old engine’s two-engines-disagree bug.
