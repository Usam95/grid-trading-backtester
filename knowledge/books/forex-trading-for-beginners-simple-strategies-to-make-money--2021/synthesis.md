# Synthesis: Forex Trading for Beginners (2021)

## 1. Bibliographic orientation (who, what, when, intended audience)
A short beginner-focused retail FX ebook from 2021 with unclear publisher provenance. It targets novice discretionary traders. See FTFB-C1-003, FTFB-C3-006, FTFB-C12-027.

## 2. Executive synthesis (<=400 words)
The book is most useful as low-credibility idea intake, not as evidence. Its reusable pieces are basic controls (FTFB-C1-002, FTFB-C1-003, FTFB-C8-020), a few formalizable entry rules (FTFB-C4-007, FTFB-C4-009, FTFB-C6-012, FTFB-C6-013, FTFB-C6-015), and operational warnings about brokers and automation (FTFB-C3-006, FTFB-C7-017, FTFB-C11-026, FTFB-C12-028). The strongest research bridge is expectancy (FTFB-C8-021). The weakest material is the passive-income framing, which is promotional and unverified (FTFB-C12-027). The crypto chapter adds little alpha and is freshness-sensitive (FTFB-C9-022).

## 3. Why useful or not (for trading/backtesting)
Useful for hypothesis generation and control design; not useful as proof. Focus on FTFB-C1-003, FTFB-C4-009, FTFB-C6-015, FTFB-C8-021, and FTFB-C11-024.

## 4. Grid-backtest relevance
Low. Only FTFB-C6-012 is adjacent; the book does not address grid spacing, inventory, or liquidation risk.

## 5. Grid live relevance
Low. Broker and automation cautions still matter: FTFB-C3-006, FTFB-C7-017, FTFB-C11-026, FTFB-C12-028.

## 6. Stock-backtest relevance
Low. A few pattern rules are portable in abstract form: FTFB-C6-013, FTFB-C6-015, FTFB-C8-021.

## 7. Stock live relevance
Low. The portable parts are mostly generic controls: FTFB-C1-003, FTFB-C8-020, FTFB-C11-024.

## 8. Shared-platform relevance
Moderate at the controls layer, low at the alpha layer. See FTFB-REQ-001 through FTFB-REQ-004.

## 9. Testable hypotheses
Use FTFB-HYP-001 through FTFB-HYP-007; the clearest are FTFB-HYP-003, FTFB-HYP-004, FTFB-HYP-006, and FTFB-HYP-007.

## 10. Research/data/simulation lessons
Expectancy is the strongest measurement lead (FTFB-C8-021). Any serious follow-up also needs cost modeling, regime labeling, and signal/broker metadata capture (FTFB-C2-005, FTFB-C7-016, FTFB-REQ-002, FTFB-REQ-004).

## 11. Execution/risk/ops lessons
The book is more valuable as a warning set than as an alpha source: FTFB-C1-002, FTFB-C3-006, FTFB-C8-019, FTFB-C8-020, FTFB-C11-026, FTFB-C12-028.

## 12. Failure modes & anti-patterns
The dominant anti-patterns are planless trading, oversized risk, stop drift, averaging down, news blindness, emotional overrides, and blind trust in brokers or robots: FTFB-C11-023 through FTFB-C12-028.

## 13. Likely obsolete/jurisdiction/venue-specific material
Broker practices, leverage norms, platform details, fee assumptions, and crypto claims all need current verification: FTFB-C3-006, FTFB-C7-017, FTFB-C9-022, FTFB-C11-026, FTFB-C12-027.

## 14. Internal contradictions
Key tensions: 24/6 versus 24/5 language, easy-profit tone versus repeated warnings, and passive-income marketing versus repeated need for supervision. See FTFB-C1-002, FTFB-C7-017, FTFB-C11-023, FTFB-C12-027.

## 15. External claims needing primary-source verification
Verify pair-liquidity claims, broker regulation, leverage limits, fee assumptions, crypto operational claims, and passive-income assertions: FTFB-C2-005, FTFB-C3-006, FTFB-C9-022, FTFB-C11-026, FTFB-C12-027.
